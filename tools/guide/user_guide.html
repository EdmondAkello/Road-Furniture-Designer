Part 1Getting started

What RoadSign Designer is for, what this edition can and cannot do yet, and how to get from a Civil 3D LandXML to a signed road in a few minutes.

1About this edition

Experimental · Demo edition · In development for HH This guide describes version {{VERSION}}, an experimental build prepared to demonstrate the workflow. Every figure comes from the Olkaria LOT 3 design alignment, used here as demonstration data. The tool is not yet released for project use: treat its proposals as a first draft for an engineer to review, not as a design.

What has been checked so far:

  • The core library has 37 automated tests and the QGIS plugin 27 headless tests (QGIS 3.34, offscreen).
  • Alignment geometry was checked against two real Civil 3D exports: Olkaria LOT 3 (UTM 37S, 59 MB with TIN surfaces and 3,569 corridor sections) and the Nessuit–Salgaa preliminary design (UTM 36S, four alignments, 26 spirals).
  • DXF georeferencing was checked on Olkaria: the reference alignment starts exactly on the LandXML's first point and every exported sign maps back to its chainage and offset with no measurable difference (section 24).
  • The copy-and-paste route was tried in Civil 3D: signs copied from the Olkaria DXF with COPYBASE and PASTEORIG landed on the alignment in the project drawing.

What has not been checked yet:

  • The auto-placement rules follow RDM 6.2, but the sight-distance and side-friction values are AASHTO 2011 defaults until they are checked against RDM Part 1.3 (section 21).
Results depend on the Civil 3D modelRoadSign Designer only knows what the LandXML tells it. Its chainages, proposals, checks and report are as good as the design work in Civil 3D and the export settings, so before relying on the output make sure that:
  • Design speeds are defined along the alignment (design criteria / speed stations), including any changes in speed zone. Where none are exported the tool assumes 80 km/h throughout.
  • Superelevation is calculated and applied to the alignment. Where it is missing, curve speeds use 4 %.
  • The design profile (finished road level) is attached to the alignment and is the final one. Without it there are no grade, crest, sag or no-overtaking checks.
  • The corridor is built with coded points (ETW, EPS, Hinge, Ditch, Daylight) and its cross-sections are sampled along the whole road. Without them signs are offset from the centreline and the corridor checks are skipped.
  • The drawing's coordinate system is set, so the LandXML carries it to QGIS and the DXF.
  • The LandXML export includes the alignment with its profiles, the design speeds and superelevation, and the corridor cross-sections. Check the dock's summary after opening a project: it lists the design speeds, road edges, vertical curves and superelevation found, and shows in red anything missing and the default used instead.

2What RoadSign Designer does

Placing traffic signs in Civil 3D and then counting them for the schedules is slow, manual work. RoadSign Designer moves both jobs into QGIS, next to the road alignment:

1 · LandXMLAlignment, profile, speeds, superelevation and corridor edges from Civil 3D.
2 · PlaceClick signs on the map, or let the engine propose them from RDM 6.2.
3 · InventoryChainage, offset, side, facing and post, kept live as signs move.
4 · ReportPlan and long-section sheets, schedule and findings (PDF, PNG, Excel).
5 · Civil 3DGeoreferenced DXF, pasted into the project drawing at the true coordinates.

Each sign is a point in a GeoPackage. Its chainage, offset, side, facing, size, grade and clearance from the road edge are worked out from the alignment and recomputed whenever the sign is moved. The inventory and the checks update as you work.

Scope:

  • Placement first. The tool does not extract signs from existing drawings, because many drawings show signs as images rather than blocks.
  • Inventory, not a Bill. Outputs are counts and chainages (Excel Tables and CSV) that feed the separate QTO and Bill process. There are no rates.
  • QGIS front end. Signs are placed on a map, not typed into a spreadsheet.
  • Civil 3D 2026+ through a georeferenced DXF. No compiled .NET add-in.

3Installing the plugin

  1. In QGIS, open Plugins → Manage and Install Plugins → Install from ZIP and choose roadsign_designer.zip.
  2. Enable RoadSign Designer. A dock opens with the Place, Inventory, Auto and Signs tabs; the toolbar button reopens it.
  3. Nothing else needs installing. The libraries used for exports (openpyxl, ezdxf and their dependencies) are pure Python and ship inside the plugin's ext_libs folder, so the plugin works where pip is blocked.
UpdatingInstall the new ZIP over the old one and restart QGIS. Existing sign projects open in newer versions; missing fields are added automatically.

4Quick start

  1. New project…: choose the LandXML, the alignment and where to save the GeoPackage. The layer CRS defaults to the LandXML's (EPSG:21037 for Olkaria).
  2. Auto tab → Propose signs. Proposed signs appear with an orange ring. Look through them, then Accept all proposals or remove the ones you do not want.
  3. Place tab: add anything the engine does not cover (junction and direction signs, place names) by picking a sign and clicking on the map.
  4. Inventory tab → Road signage report: choose the plan scale and fill in Prepared / Checked / Approved by.
  5. Inventory tab → Export to Civil 3D: choose the plan scale, then paste the signs into the project drawing (section 16).

Part 2Working in QGIS

Creating a sign project, placing and editing signs, writing legends, and the checks that run as you work.

5A sign project from a LandXML

New project… asks for the LandXML, the alignment to sign (a file can hold several), and a GeoPackage to save. The GeoPackage is the master record: it holds the signs and a copy of everything derived from the LandXML that the map needs.

Layers added to the map:

LayerContents
RSD signsOne point per sign face, drawn with its RDM artwork (faces on one post are stacked).
RSD alignmentThe signed alignment.
RSD chainageTicks and labels every 100 m.
RSD road edgesCorridor edges from the LandXML cross-sections, coloured by code (ETW, EPS, Hinge, Ditch_In, Ditch_Out, Daylight).
RSD vertical curvesCrest and sag PVIs with length, K and A.

The sign layer takes the LandXML's coordinate system when the file declares one. If the QGIS project uses a different CRS the plugin says so; QGIS reprojects on the fly, but keeping both the same avoids confusion when exchanging with Civil 3D.

6Placing signs

  1. Pick a sign from the palette. Filter by category or search by code or name.
  2. Size. Auto (RDM, by design speed) applies the manual's size tables using the design speed at the sign's chainage. Choose a size to override it.
  3. Mirrored gives the left/right version of signs the manual shows one way round (bends, side roads, chevrons).
  4. Facing. Auto follows driving on the left: a sign on the left faces up-chainage traffic, a sign on the right faces down-chainage traffic. Choose Forward or Backward to override, for example in a median.
  5. Offset from a road edge (EPS by default) and Fix offset at place the post at a set distance beyond that edge at the chainage you click. The live readout shows, for example, +1.00 m from EPS grade +2.5%.
  6. Keep inside the corridor (on by default) snaps the post into RDM 6.2 §2.4's band, 0.6 to 3.0 m beyond the edge, and never beyond the ditch or daylight line where those are modelled.
  7. Add to an existing post: click an existing sign to put another face (a plate, a back-to-back sign) on the same post.
  8. Place signs on map. Hovering shows chainage and offset; left-click places, right-click stops.

7Legends, plates and values

Directional signs, town and county signs and text plates carry text that changes from job to job: a sign at Salgaa reads Nakuru and Eldoret, not Mombasa. Type the legend before placing, or select the sign on the Signs tab and use Edit legend…. One destination per line:

^ Olkaria 6              ahead, 6 km
< Naivasha B3 32         left, route B3, 32 km
> Narok B3 120           right
  • Arrows: ^ or A ahead, < or L left, > or R right. A line with no arrow is plain text (plates).
  • Destinations are ordered as RDM §5.2 asks: ahead, then left, then right, furthest first.
  • Faces are drawn in the RDM Table 2.6 colours for the road class: green with yellow route numbers (A, B, UA), white (C, D, UC and lower), blue (expressway).
  • Signs whose artwork carries a value take it from the legend. A W30 steep-descent sign proposed for an 11 % grade reads 11 %, not the manual's 10 % sample, on the map, in the report and in the DXF.

8Moving and editing signs

  • Move signs with the normal QGIS editing tools (Toggle Editing → Move Feature). Chainage, offset, side, auto-facing, size, grade and edge clearance update at once, and every face on the same post moves with it.
  • Editing facing in the attribute table switches Auto facing off for that sign; typing a size switches Auto size off.
  • The Signs tab lists every face in chainage order. Double-click a row to zoom to it. Renumber posts by chainage renames posts P001, P002… in road order.

Part 3Auto-placement

The engine reads the alignment's geometry and proposes the warning and regulatory signs RDM 6.2 calls for. Proposals are suggestions: an engineer accepts or removes each one.

10What the engine signs

Signs are placed at the advance distance (6 seconds of travel at the design speed, RDM 6.2 §2.4), on the left for up-chainage traffic and on the right for down-chainage traffic, at the set clearance beyond the reference edge, and moved apart where they would be closer than 0.6 V metres.

On Olkaria LOT 3 (80 km/h) the engine reports 36 findings and proposes 92 faces in about 2.5 s: five curves of R 100–110 m with a safe speed near 55 km/h, descents to 10.8 %, and three short crests (K = 10, 16 and 14).

11Running and reviewing proposals

  1. On the Auto tab, tick the rules to run and set the chainage range (the whole alignment by default).
  2. Propose signs. Proposals are added to the sign layer with status proposed, drawn with an orange ring, and listed with the reason for each (for example R100 L: curve speed 55 vs 80 km/h).
  3. Review on the map and in the report, which shows proposals too. Move, edit or delete individual proposals as needed.
  4. Accept all proposals turns the remainder into ordinary signs. Remove proposals deletes all that are still proposed.
ExportsThe inventory and the Civil 3D exports include accepted and hand-placed signs only. The report includes proposals, ringed, so they can be reviewed on paper.

Part 4Outputs

The inventory for the QTO, the road signage report, and the two routes into Civil 3D drawings.

13Inventory for the QTO

Export inventory (Excel + CSV) writes *_Inventory.xlsx with three named Excel Tables, ready to link into a QTO workbook, and a CSV of the faces:

TableOne row perMain columns

There are no rates: the tables feed the separate QTO and Bill process.

14Road signage report

Road signage report (PDF + PNG sheets + Excel) asks for:

  • Plan scale for the plan layout sheets: Auto (1:2500, or 1:1000 for roads under 1.2 km), a fixed scale from 1:500 to 1:5000, or no plan sheets.
  • Prepared by, Checked by, Approved by for the title blocks. The names are kept with the project and offered again next time.

It writes an A3 landscape PDF, a PNG per plan and long-section sheet, and an Excel workbook. Every page sits in the same frame: a border, the title with the HH logo at the top right, and a title block with Project, Alignment, Drawing, Scale, Prepared / Checked / Approved by, Date and Sheet. To use another logo, point the QGIS setting RoadSignDesigner/report_logo at an image file.

Plan layout sheets

  • Each sheet is turned so the road runs left to right. Sheet lengths adapt to the road: on straights a sheet covers about 950 m at 1:2500; on tight curves it is shortened so the road and the signs either side still fit. Olkaria's 16.7 km fits on 18 sheets.
  • Signs are drawn upright at 10 mm, as in HH plan drawings, beside the road with a leader to the post. A sign and its plate stack; back-to-back faces sit side by side; chevrons are drawn at 70 % so a series stays readable. Symbols are moved along or away from the road until they clear one another.
  • The LandXML's other alignments (side and link roads) are drawn and named.
  • Match lines carry the chainage and the neighbouring sheet number.

Long-section sheets

One sheet per kilometre shows the signs for each direction of travel, the design profile (and existing ground where the LandXML has it), steep grades, no-overtaking zones per direction, crests and sags with K values, sight-distance findings, the curvature band with each curve's radius and safe speed, and the grades between PVIs. Labels are staggered so that close signs, such as a chevron series, never overlap.

Schedule, findings and workbook

The Excel workbook has the sheets Signs (with proposals and the reason for each), Findings, Horizontal curves (R, e, f, safe speed against design speed), Vertical curves (K, A, SSD) and Basis (the criteria used).

15DXF for Civil 3D drawings

Export to Civil 3D (package + DXF) asks for the plan scale (default 1:2500) and writes the placement package (*_placement.json) and a DXF of the placed signs.

  • Size. Symbols print at 10 mm, matching HH drawings: 25 m in model space at 1:2500, 10 m at 1:1000. Codes, legends and chainages print at 1.8 mm.
  • One block per sign, at the post. Each sign is a block RSDC_<code>_<id> inserted at the post's true coordinates. Inside it are a post marker, a leader, the sign face upright (the RDM artwork block) and the code underneath. Moving, copying or reading back acts on the post.
  • Text signs. Directional signs and plates get their own block with the real legend (colours, arrows, place names, route numbers, distances) at 0.6 of the symbol size.
  • Attributes and identity. Every sign block carries SIGN_CODE, LEGEND, DESCRIPTION, SIZE, CHAINAGE, OFFSET, SIDE, FACING, POST_REF, FACE_UUID and INSTALL_UUID, with the UUIDs repeated in XDATA (application RSD).
  • Reference geometry (non-plotting): the signed alignment with chainages, every other alignment in the LandXML, the corridor edges by code, and a coordinate check point.

16Copy and paste at the true coordinates

The DXF is in the LandXML's own world coordinates: easting and northing in metres, with no local origin, shift, scale or rotation. The header records metres as the unit, angles counter-clockwise from east, and custom variables RSD_CRS, RSD_MAP_ZONE, RSD_SOURCE_LANDXML, RSD_ALIGNMENT and RSD_COORDINATES. Extents are set, so Zoom Extents frames the road.

  1. Open *_placement.dxf in Civil 3D.
  2. Select the signs: QSELECT → Layer = RSD-SIGN.
  3. COPYBASE with base point 0,0,0.
  4. In the project drawing, run PASTEORIG. The signs land at their true coordinates.

Alternatively, INSERT or XREF the whole DXF at 0,0,0, scale 1, rotation 0. The project drawing must be in metres, use the same coordinate system and be in the World UCS.

Check pointThe layer RSD-REF-NOTES holds a cross at the start of the alignment labelled with its easting and northing. Paste it once to confirm it falls on the Civil 3D alignment, then delete it.

17Dynamo link (parked)

Dynamo scripts that place native blocks from the placement package and read moves back into QGIS are kept in the repository (dynamo/), but this route is parked for now. Use the georeferenced DXF (section 16) to bring signs into Civil 3D.

Part 5Technical manual

How the data is stored, what is read from the LandXML, the geometry and design formulas, the catalogue, the plan-layout method, and how the outputs were verified.

18Data model

A sign project is one GeoPackage:

TableContents
rsd_signsPoint per sign face. User fields and derived fields (Appendix A).
rsd_metaKey/value: LandXML path (relative), alignment, project name, schema, CRS of the LandXML, layer CRS, Prepared / Checked / Approved by.
rsd_alignment, rsd_chainageAlignment line and chainage ticks.
rsd_edgesCorridor edges as lines, split where sections are missing.
rsd_vcurvesVertical curve PVIs with kind, length, K, A and grades.

Faces on one post share an install_uuid; each face has its own face_uuid. The UUIDs follow a sign through the inventory, the package, the DXF and any read-back, so the same sign is recognised everywhere. Derived fields are recomputed from the alignment when a sign moves or when a field that affects them changes; they are never typed.

19What is read from the LandXML

ElementUsed for
CoordinateSystemEPSG code and name: layer CRS, DXF header.

Feature SpeedStation, Roadway/DesignSpeedDesign speed by chainage: sizes, advance distances, sight distances.
Superelevation / FullSupereleve for curve speeds.
Profile / ProfAlignPVIs and parabolic curves: grades, K, crests, sags, sight distance.
Profile / ProfSurfExisting ground on the long sections.

The file is streamed (iterparse), and TIN surfaces are skipped, so large exports load quickly: Olkaria's 59 MB file opens in about 1.3 s.

20Geometry methods

  • Point at station and offset. Each element gives its point and heading in closed form (lines, arcs) or by numerical integration of curvature (clothoids). Positive offsets are to the right of increasing chainage.
  • Station and offset of a point. The alignment is densified, the nearest segment found, and the result refined on the element itself.
  • Road edges. For each code and side, the section offsets are interpolated along the road. An edge is treated as absent across a gap longer than three times its usual section spacing (at least 30 m), so a daylight line modelled only in cut is not bridged across fills.
  • Corridor clamp. The post is held 0.6–3.0 m beyond the reference edge; the outer corridor limit (ditch or daylight) applies only where the verge is actually modelled.
  • Grade at a sign. From the design profile, including inside vertical curves.

22The sign catalogue

  • Source. tools/extract_rdm_signs.py reads RDM 6.2 (2025), matches each figure caption to its framed artwork, keeps the sign and drops dimension lines and notes, and writes an SVG icon and a CAD block per sign (RSD_<code>, colour hatches). tools/build_catalogue.py adds sizes, legend types and size rules.
  • Coverage. R, M, P, W, I, E, PD, IM and RW signs, 245 in all. Signs used both ways round have a mirrored version (_M). W24 chevrons are split into W24-a to W24-d.
  • Numbering clashes in the manual, resolved as follows: Fig 7.1 captioned P1 becomes PD1; I10 used twice, so County Boundary becomes I10B; IM4 used twice, so the expressway symbol becomes IM4S.
  • Other notes. Table 5.1 lists UC under both green and white faces (Table 2.6 is followed). Table 4.1's siting distances (1000, 900 and 600 m) look implausible, so the §2.4 six-second rule is used.
  • Coding. RSD uses RDM 6.2 codes, and HH drawings will carry them (agreed November 2026). The Ministry's standard drawings WSCM/84/2/01 (2024) number signs differently.

23Plan layout of symbols and sheets

At drawing scale a sign symbol is far bigger than its post (25 m at 1:2500), so drawings show it beside the road with a leader. The same method places symbols in the DXF and on the plan sheets:

  1. Faces are grouped by post. Faces with the same facing stack top to bottom (sign over plate); back-to-back faces sit side by side. Each face's box includes room for its label.
  2. Groups are taken in chainage order. Each is tried at candidate positions just outside its post, away from the road, then slid along the road (ahead first) and only then moved further out, until its box clears every box already placed.
  3. The leader runs from the post to the nearest point of the group's box.

Plan sheets are cut so the road fits: starting from the sheet's full length at the chosen scale, the window is shortened by 10 % at a time until the road, turned to run along the sheet, fits the viewport with room for symbols either side. A short remainder at the end of the road is merged into the last sheet.

25Limitations and roadmap

  • Civil 3D. The DXF paste works; the Dynamo link is parked.
  • HH drawing conventions. Symbol sizes now match the 25 m blocks in current HH drawings at 1:2500. Block names, layers and annotation style should be matched to HH's once a DXF export of a current drawing is available.
  • Quantities. Post and foundation types for the QTO are still to be agreed. Sign numbering follows RDM 6.2.
  • Corridor restriction. Implemented as RDM's 0.6–3.0 m band inside the ditch or daylight line. A road-reserve boundary is not used yet.
  • Junctions. Junction signing (RDM Table A1-1) is not automated; place those signs by hand.
  • Sight distances use AASHTO 2011 defaults until checked against RDM Part 1.3.
